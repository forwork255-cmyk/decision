## What We Found
- Cost of trying is trivial ($5, no installation) versus the recurring cost of forgetting your umbrella (getting soaked, wasted time going back) — the downside is capped and the upside is real. (Contrarian)
- The stated cause ("blends into the wall") is a plausible mechanism for visual-blindness/habituation, and color contrast is a well-known low-cost fix for exactly this kind of "walked past it a hundred times" problem — reasonable to act on without further proof. (Executor)
- 30-day return window removes most of the remaining risk if the insert doesn't actually change your behavior (e.g., if the real cause is being rushed, not visibility).
- Survival check: Does the verdict survive if the orange insert doesn't fully fix the habituation problem? Yes — because the cost of testing it is $5 and reversible, so even a moderate chance of failure doesn't change the "try it" answer.

## Strongest Evidence For
Near-zero cost, no installation, fully returnable — this is closer to a free experiment than a real commitment.

## Strongest Evidence Against
It's possible the forgetting isn't purely a visibility problem (e.g., rushing out the door, not looking down at all) — in which case color won't help and the $5 (plus the friction of remembering to return it) is mildly wasted.

## Biggest Risk
That the real cause is attention/routine, not color-blindness-to-the-object — meaning the insert doesn't fix the behavior and you're back to forgetting it, just with a more colorful item you're now also failing to notice. Nothing else stands out as missed by the two perspectives used here — this is a low-stakes decision without much for an adversarial pass to find.

## What Would Change the Verdict?
- Does changing the umbrella's visual contrast with its surroundings actually change whether you notice it on your way out? (causal)
- If you tried a different low-cost cue instead (e.g., moving it to hang on the door handle itself) would that work better — not decision-changing at $5, since testing the insert first is essentially free anyway.
